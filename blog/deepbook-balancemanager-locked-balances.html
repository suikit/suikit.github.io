DeepBook V3 BalanceManager — free vs locked vs settled, and where your balance actually is

Your bot checks the wallet: 1,000 SUI ready. It places an order — abort, insufficient balance. Or worse: orders fill, days pass, and the proceeds never seem to come back. Both symptoms come from the same misunderstanding: on DeepBook V3 your trading funds don't live in your wallet. They live in a BalanceManager, and they exist in three different states.

Free, locked, settled

A BalanceManager is the on-chain object that holds the funds your orders are backed by. Every coin it tracks sits in exactly one of three states:

  • Free — available. Backs new orders, or can be withdrawn to your wallet.
  • Locked — committed to open orders right now. You still own it, but it isn't spendable until those orders are cancelled or filled. This is the state that breaks bots that only read free.
  • Settled — proceeds of fills sitting in limbo. Not locked, but not free either: nothing moves until you explicitly call withdraw_settled_amounts.

Where it goes wrong in practice

1. Reading only free. A bot that sizes orders off the free balance will under-size forever once a grid of open orders locks most of the funds — and conversely, one that reads total balance will try to spend money that's already committed. Reconcile free + locked against your open orders, not against your wallet.

2. Forgetting settled. This is the silent leak. Fills accumulate in the settled bucket and just sit there — your NAV is fine but your deployable balance keeps shrinking. The fix is to sweep settled funds in the same transaction as your order management:

// inside the same PTB as cancel + place
tx.moveCall({
  target: `${PKG}::pool::withdraw_settled_amounts`,
  arguments: [tx.object(config.id), managerObj, proof],
  typeArguments: [config.baseType, config.quoteType],
});

Chaining cancel_all → place_limit_order → withdraw_settled_amounts in one transaction turns settlement into a non-issue: every rebalance also recycles the proceeds of whatever filled.

3. Locked DEEP is still DEEP. The fee balance is governed by the same rules — DEEP committed to fee payment on open orders counts as locked, which is why a manager holding 50 DEEP can still hit MoveAbort code 3 on a 0.5 DEEP fee.

4. Funds stranded across pools. Open orders on a pool you no longer trade keep their collateral locked. Periodic "satellite cleanup" — cancel stale orders on non-core pools and withdraw — is required ops, not an edge case.

Operational rules we landed on

  • Size against free, reconcile against open orders. The manager state, not the wallet, is the source of truth.
  • Sweep settled in the same tx as cancel+place — never as a separate periodic job that can be skipped.
  • Treat locked as spent for risk purposes: it's backing orders that can fill.
  • Clean up satellite pools on a schedule, not when you notice drift.

Result

Once settled funds are swept automatically and orders size against the real free balance, "missing money" bugs disappear entirely — the manager's state machine does exactly what it says, your bot just has to read all three columns.
